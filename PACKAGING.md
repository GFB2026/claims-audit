# Packaging (how this could be sold)

Not a live product yet — this is the shape a paid engagement would take.

## Positioning

**Independently verify what someone is telling you, before you rely on it.**

The engine (deep-research + 3-vote adversarial verification, every claim checked by reviewers instructed to *disprove* it first) is category-agnostic. The claim schema and search angles are what change between lanes:

| Lane | Buyer checks | Example claim schema |
|---|---|---|
| Vendor/procurement diligence | Is this SaaS vendor's security/uptime/customer story real before we sign? | security & compliance, company viability, customer references, product/technical, support & pricing |
| Ad/marketing claims substantiation | Can legal defend this ad copy if challenged? | quantitative claims, comparative claims, testimonial/endorsement claims |
| Executive/founder background check | Does this person's claimed track record hold up? | employment history, credentialed roles, quantified achievements |
| Competitive-intel monitoring | What changed in a competitor's public claims this quarter? | same schema, re-run on a cadence |

## Tiers (illustrative, vendor-diligence lane)

| Tier | Scope | Turnaround |
|---|---|---|
| Quick Scan | 2 highest-risk categories | 24-48 hrs |
| Full Audit | All 5 categories | 48-72 hrs |
| Renewal Watch | Full Audit re-run on a cadence | recurring |

## Why the model works

Cost is compute/time, not headcount, so it scales without hiring. The adversarial-vote structure (not a single pass of "does this sound plausible") is what makes the output trustworthy enough to act on — most manual vendor-risk reviews take a vendor's own questionnaire at face value; this doesn't.
